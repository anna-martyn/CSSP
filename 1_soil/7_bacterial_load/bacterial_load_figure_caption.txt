Fig. SX. Bacterial load in rhizosphere and root samples of Lotus japonicus wild-type and CSSP mutant plants grown in Askov soils. Synthetic spike-in DNA (10⁵ copies) was added to the first PCR of each sample. (A) Number of spike-in reads per sample. One root sample (PK, nsp2) consisted almost entirely of spike-in reads (99.1%; 387 bacterial reads). It is included in (A) but was excluded from load estimation (B, C). (B) Total bacterial load, calculated as the ratio of bacterial to spike-in reads multiplied by the number of spike-in copies. (C) Commensal bacterial load, calculated as total load multiplied by the relative abundance of commensal ASVs. Symbionts, defined as Rhizobiales ASVs with ≥1% mean relative abundance in wild-type nodules of the respective soil, were excluded. (D) As in C, but with all ASVs with ≥1% mean relative abundance in wild-type nodules (including non-rhizobial co-colonisers such as Pseudomonas) excluded. Dots represent biological replicates (n = 6, except PK root: ccamk n = 5, nsp2 n = 5 in A) and n = 4 in B-D). Mutants were compared with the wild-type within each soil and compartment using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction (adjusted P ≥ 0.05, ns = non-significant). Y-axes are log₁₀-scaled.
